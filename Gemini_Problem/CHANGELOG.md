## 2026-08-25

### Methodological clarification: Controlling Chronology Standard

The archive formally adopted the following rule:

> The chronological sequence documented in the primary artifacts is treated as an observed fact to the extent that each component of that sequence is directly supported by the preserved evidence.

The archive currently documents a sequence involving:

1. Generation of contested language in the preserved conversation.
2. Subsequent user confrontation concerning that language.
3. Later unavailability of response-side material in visible interfaces while user-side prompts and attachments remained represented.

This sequence is preserved as a chronological observation.

### Clarification regarding causation

The causal relationship, if any, between:

- the contested content;
- the subsequent confrontation;
- and the later unavailability of response-side material

is not established by the currently available evidence.

Possible mechanisms remain open.

These may include:

- technical retrieval failure;
- synchronization failure;
- indexing failure;
- filtering;
- backend processing;
- session or account-state divergence;
- retention differences;
- intentional intervention;
- human review;
- other unidentified causes.

No mechanism is adopted as the established explanation without evidence that specifically distinguishes it from competing alternatives.

### Purpose of the clarification

This clarification was adopted to prevent two opposite errors:

1. **Dismissing a documented chronological pattern because causation remains unresolved.**
2. **Converting chronology alone into proof of motive, intent, human involvement, censorship, deletion, or a specific causal mechanism.**

The controlling rule is therefore:

> **Preserve the documented sequence without dismissing it, and do not elevate chronology alone into proof of causation.**

## 2026-08-25

### Evidence-category clarification

The archive formalized the following four-category evidentiary structure:

#### Observed

Directly present in primary artifacts, including:

- transcripts;
- screenshots;
- visible text;
- timestamps;
- metadata;
- file names;
- attachment records;
- interface states.

#### Independently Documented

Information established by external sources, including:

- official documentation;
- official privacy policies;
- official support materials;
- independently identifiable public reports.

The existence of a documented mechanism does not establish that it occurred in this specific case.

#### Inferred

Bounded interpretations or deductions derived from observed and independently documented information.

Inferences must remain distinguishable from direct observation.

#### Possible / Unknown

Unverified hypotheses or mechanisms.

Where the available evidence does not discriminate between competing explanations, the correct status remains:

> **UNKNOWN**

## 2026-08-25

### Primary-source preservation rule

The archive established the following preservation policy:

- Original transcripts remain preserved as primary artifacts.
- Screenshots remain preserved in their original form.
- Raw logs remain preserved.
- Original files are not silently rewritten to conform to later analysis.
- Later summaries and interpretations may be revised.
- Corrections to methodology are recorded in the changelog.

The purpose of this distinction is to preserve provenance.

---

## 2026-08-25

### AI-generated analysis classification

The archive clarified that AI-generated responses may themselves be preserved as artifacts.

A preserved AI response can establish that a particular model generated particular language within a documented interaction.

However, model-generated claims concerning:

- internal architecture;
- training;
- hidden instructions;
- corporate practices;
- human review;
- deletion mechanisms;
- system behavior outside the visible interaction

are not automatically treated as independently verified technical facts.

The controlling distinction is:

> **The model said it**  
> does not automatically mean  
> **The statement has been independently verified.**

---

## 2026-08-25

### Cross-model comparison clarification

The archive may contain analyses from multiple AI systems.

Cross-model analysis may assist with:

- inventory;
- file organization;
- contradiction detection;
- interpretation testing;
- portability of the evidence corpus.

However, agreement between multiple AI systems analyzing the same underlying artifacts is not treated as independent corroboration of the underlying events.

The primary artifacts remain the central evidentiary sources.

# Current Controlling Principle

The archive currently operates under the following rule:

> **Preserve what is documented. Attribute what is sourced. Label what is inferred. Leave unresolved mechanisms unresolved.**

And:

> **The documented chronology must not be dismissed, but chronology alone must not be converted into proof of motive, intent, or mechanism.**
