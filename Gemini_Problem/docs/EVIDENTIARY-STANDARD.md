# Evidentiary Standard

**Status:** Controlling methodology document  
**Applies to:** All analysis, summaries, chronology documents, and future additions to this archive

---

## Purpose

This archive preserves primary artifacts and distinguishes them from later interpretation.

The purpose of this standard is to prevent two opposite errors:

1. Dismissing documented evidence merely because the causal mechanism remains unresolved.
2. Converting documented evidence into conclusions that the evidence does not independently establish.

The archive therefore separates observation, independently documented information, inference, and unresolved possibilities.

---

# The Four Evidence Categories

## 1. Observed

**Observed** means directly present in a primary artifact.

This includes, where applicable:

- text contained in a preserved transcript;
- visible text in screenshots;
- timestamps;
- dates;
- file names;
- attachment indicators;
- document metadata;
- visible interface states;
- visible error messages;
- other information directly represented in the preserved artifact.

Observed statements should be capable of being checked directly against the source material.

Observed does not mean:

- explained;
- caused;
- intentional;
- representative of a broader mechanism;
- independently verified beyond the artifact itself.

### Example

> A screenshot displays user-side prompts and attachment indicators while corresponding response-side text is not visible in that representation.

That is an observation about what the screenshot displays.

It does not establish why the response-side text is absent.

---

## 2. Independently Documented

**Independently Documented** means information established by a source outside the primary archive.

Examples may include:

- official Google privacy policies;
- official Google support documentation;
- official product documentation;
- independently identifiable public reports;
- published technical documentation.

Independently documented information must remain attributed to its source.

A general documented mechanism does not establish that the mechanism occurred in this specific incident.
